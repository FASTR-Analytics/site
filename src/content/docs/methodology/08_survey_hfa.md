## Background and purpose

### Objective of the module

The Health Facility Assessment (HFA) survey analysis module complements routine HMIS data by collecting information directly from health facilities through phone surveys. While routine data provides continuous monitoring of service delivery volumes, phone surveys assess gaps in primary care service delivery and capture additional dimensions that are not available in administrative systems, including service availability, facility readiness, the impact of shocks on primary health care performance, challenges in service provision, and other facility characteristics.

FASTR's rapid-cycle HFA phone survey is designed to be low-cost, flexible, and to support primary care system monitoring by increasing the systematic and timely use of health facility data for decision-making. By contacting primary health care facilities directly, the survey gathers real-time information on operational status, staffing, supply availability, and service provision that can be triangulated with routine HMIS data to provide actionable information on health service delivery.

### What are rapid-cycle health facility phone surveys?

Effective PHC systems are critical to scaling up high-impact interventions to improve RMNCAH-N outcomes. Rapid-cycle longitudinal phone surveys conducted with health care facilities establish an ongoing monitoring platform, enabling countries to:

1. Continuously evaluate PHC readiness and identify obstacles to delivering high-quality care, with feedback loops to inform strengthening actions at multiple levels
2. Keep a close watch on the implementation of health care reforms, providing the insights needed to course-correct or scale up implementation
3. Capture the effects of unexpected events, such as epidemics, natural disasters, or violence, on the health system, allowing the PHC system's resilience to be measured in near-real time
4. Track changes in health system performance over time

### Analytical rationale

Routine HMIS data tells us *how much* service is being delivered, but not *why* delivery patterns may be changing. Facility phone surveys help answer questions that routine data cannot address:

- Are essential supplies, vaccines, and medicines available to meet service demands?
- Are trained staff present?
- What challenges are facilities facing, and how do they affect service delivery?
- What quality improvement processes are in place?
- How does the facility engage with the community it serves?

By linking survey responses to routine data patterns, the FASTR approach enables a more nuanced interpretation of service delivery trends. Joint analysis can help decision makers understand how service availability and readiness relate to service utilization, disruption, and coverage. Facility-level and geographic disaggregations, such as comparing levels of care, or regions affected by priority projects or reforms with those not affected, tell a more comprehensive story of how reform efforts are affecting the supply and demand sides of the health system.

## Survey methodology

### Sampling approach

The FASTR HFA uses a stratified random sample of primary care health facilities followed longitudinally over time, with stratification typically based on:

1. **Geographic region** - Ensuring representation across provinces/districts, where possible
2. **Facility type** - District hospitals, health centers, health posts
3. **Facility ownership** - Public, private, faith-based
4. **HMIS reporting status** - Active reporters vs. non-reporters

Sample size is determined based on the precision required for key indicators and the resources available for data collection.

### Data collection

Facility phone surveys are conducted by trained enumerators using a standardized questionnaire. Key features include:

- **Respondent**: Facility in-charge or designated health worker
- **Duration**: 30-45 minutes per facility
- **Frequency**: Quarterly or as needed by the country
- **Quality control**: Call-backs, supervisor monitoring, data validation

### Validity of phone surveys

Phone surveys have been validated against in-person surveys and give consistent results. In a comparison in Ghana (n = 63 facilities), there was no significant difference in mean scores between the phone and in-person approaches, and item-level checks such as mean staffing numbers showed no significant difference. Most items perform strongly by phone; a few supply- and infrastructure-related items are better verified in person and can be flagged for a follow-up visit.

### Questionnaire structure

The standard FASTR HFA questionnaire is completed over the course of four quarterly contacts with a panel of health facilities. It covers ten core modules: three are collected every quarter, and the remaining seven are each implemented annually, at one survey round. Questions are drawn from the WHO Primary Health Care Measurement Framework and Indicators (PHCMFI) and harmonized with the HHFA, SARA, SPA, and SDI facility-assessment tools. The final questionnaire structure and module design are customized by each country and may differ from country to country.

| Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 | Round 4 |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Facility characteristics | Facility characteristics\* | Facility characteristics\* | Facility characteristics\* |
| Shocks and resilience | Shocks and resilience | Shocks and resilience | Shocks and resilience |
| Medical supplies | Medical supplies | Medical supplies | Medical supplies |
| Infrastructure (key interruption questions only) | Infrastructure (key interruption questions only) | Infrastructure (full module) | Infrastructure (key interruption questions only) |
| Service availability | Financing | Quality improvement processes | Workforce and staffing |
| | Community engagement | | Leadership and coordination |

\* Asked only to replacement facilities. Additional locally relevant questions are added during questionnaire adaptation.

**Quarterly modules**

- Shocks and resilience
- Medical supplies
- Infrastructure (key interruption questions; the full module once a year)

**Annual modules**

- Facility characteristics
- Service availability
- Financing
- Community engagement
- Quality improvement processes
- Workforce and staffing
- Leadership and coordination

Optional modules include emergency preparedness, immunization (with Gavi), ANC operational capacity, and delivery/EmONC operational capacity.

## Questionnaire adaptation

### Adaptation guidelines

The standard FASTR HFA survey questionnaire serves as a starting point that should be adapted to each country context. Key adaptation considerations:

1. **Language** - Translation and back-translation
2. **Health system terminology** - Align with local naming conventions
3. **Indicator priorities** - Prioritize nationally relevant services
4. **Skip patterns** - Adjust based on facility types and relevance in the country
5. **Response options** - Match local context (e.g., supply names)

### Adaptation process

**Step 1: Identify country priorities**
Determine which indicators are most important for the country's objectives.

**Step 2: Review the standard questionnaire**
Walk through each section with country stakeholders to identify what works and what needs modification. Adaptation guidance is provided directly on the questionnaire, highlighting which questions most often require country-specific changes.

**Step 3: Adapt the questionnaire**
Contextualize wording, add country-specific questions, remove irrelevant items.

**Step 4: Pre-test**
Test the adapted questionnaire with a small sample of facilities.

**Step 5: Finalize**
Incorporate pre-test feedback and prepare the final version for data collection.

## Survey-routine data linkage

### Linking survey responses to HMIS data

A key feature of the FASTR approach is comparing phone survey responses to routine HMIS data. This includes aggregate comparison of findings at national or subnational levels as well as linked analysis of the same facilities. These analyses enable:

1. **Validation** - Check whether survey-reported service availability matches HMIS reporting patterns
2. **Contextualization** - Understand possible reasons *why* routine data shows certain patterns
3. **Triangulation** - Compare multiple data sources for the same facilities to gain a fuller picture

### Use cases for linked analysis

- **Non-reporting facilities**: Survey confirms whether a facility is closed or just not reporting
- **Service volume changes**: Survey reveals how changes in coverage relate to service delivery factors such as equipment availability, stockouts of medicines, or staffing
- **Quality interpretation**: Survey data adds context to routine data quality flags

---

## Data use and decision-making

### Using HFA results

Survey results should inform:

1. **Routine data interpretation** - Explain patterns in HMIS data
2. **Facility readiness** - Identify areas with poor readiness for actionable responses
3. **Supply chain management** - Track stockout patterns
4. **Health system strengthening** - Inform broader system improvements
